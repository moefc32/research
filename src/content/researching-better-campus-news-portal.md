Have you ever tried to look up campus events and felt lost trying to figure out where the real updates are? That was the question that pushed me to investigate how university news portals actually worked. At Telkom University, we had 55+ student activity units that constantly needed to share non-academic events, yet three official media channels already existed to accommodate them. The interesting part was that students still felt information dissemination wasn't optimal, which made me question why the existing channels weren't working as well as they should.

Campus life moves fast, and staying updated on student activities is essential. However, the digital landscape for campus news was fragmented. While platforms like student portals and journalism sites were running, students still felt that information dissemination was far from optimal. To understand why this gap existed, our research team looked closely at how media platforms were managed and why updates were missing the mark.

## The Investigation

I didn't want to evaluate the problem only from a website design perspective. The way students received information involved journalism, technology, social media, and the actual experience of the audience, so our research needed to look at the problem from several sides. We began by observing visual layouts and user interfaces across seven different university news websites, then conducted in-depth interviews with experts in journalism, web technology, social media management, and user interface design.

Building on those qualitative insights, we also distributed questionnaires to 120 student respondents to measure their actual satisfaction levels and daily expectations. Finally, we processed all the collected data through a comparative matrix to bring the different perspectives together and identify the requirements for a better campus news portal.

## The Findings

What became clear to me was that building a trusted student news portal wasn't simply a matter of making a better-looking website. Based on the research data, media managers needed reliable networks for gathering and distributing accurate information quickly, while the platform itself needed to support citizen journalism, work well across mobile devices, and use secure official campus domains to strengthen user trust.

These findings gave me a more grounded understanding of what a campus news platform needed to accomplish. I shared the results with people involved in student media, but their value extended further for me because they became a reference point for developing student news portals in later work. Having already spent considerable time working with student media, I had an advantage in understanding the environment, and the research helped turn that practical experience into something more systematic that I could carry into future projects.
